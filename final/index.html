Best features

Live from the push feed

ESPN's own websocket, plus a cache-skipping check every second, so a play lands the moment ESPN publishes it. Lag behind the real play is measured and shown on the page.

Fair by design

Timed markets open only in a window that closes before the next snap is likely. A pick places or fails at the tap. Nothing is voided after the fact.

Book-real prices

Game lines are the book's live prices. Alt lines, team totals, quarters, next play, drive and next score come from models fit on 2023-2025 play-by-play, each with a stated hold.

Every play is a decision

Next play, moves the chains, this drive and next score reopen after each snap, with a countdown chip on every market.

The Rush loop

Weekly Stack, Units, the Field ranked live, a named head-to-head rival, rank alerts when you pass someone.

Game context in one place

Scorebug with timeouts, field and drive, storylines rail, box score with headshots, injuries with returned or did-not-return, the Lounge.

How a player moves through a game

1
Board to event

Taps Bears at Seahawks on the Board. The event opens in Live with the remaining weekly Stack and open picks already in the Slip.

2
Read the moment

Scorebug, drive and the storylines rail say what matters now: the underdog, the red zone, a player 30 yards from a milestone.

3
Pick in two taps

Tap a price, set a stake, lock it. One-tap stakes for next play. A countdown says how long the market stays open.

4
Sweat it

The Slip shows a live meter and whether the price moved your way since you locked it.

5
Result and rank

Hit or miss lands seconds after ESPN posts the play. The Field reorders, the rival card updates, the Lounge reacts.

6
Next snap

The window reopens. The loop runs about every 40 seconds for three hours.

What the simulated Field said

Survey results from the chief of staff go here.

Gaps still open

  • Data speed is the ceiling. ESPN's free feed lands about 20s after the play, so next-play markets often cannot open after a run or catch. The book's own feed beats ESPN but still arrived about 14s after the snap tonight.
  • The Field is simulated. SIM players stand in for real players. Shared picks, chat and ranks need the Rush backend.
  • Props lean on models. The fits beat a baseline on 2025 data, but yards overs read slightly low. Book prop prices would be better.
  • No real-phone test yet. Checked at phone size in a browser, not on an iPhone in the hand.

With more time

  • An official low-latency data feed (a Rush purchase decision), so markets lock off the snap itself and stay open after every play.
  • The real Field: shared picks, ranks and the Lounge on the Rush backend, with push notifications for rank changes and hits.
  • Book prop prices through SportsGameOdds behind a small server proxy that holds the key.
  • A live parlay builder and the Watch door wired into provider apps for casting.
